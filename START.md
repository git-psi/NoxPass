# Hello, and welcome to NoxPass! 🦉

Hi there, and thank you for trying NoxPass. I hope you enjoy it as much as I enjoyed building it.

This little guide takes about five minutes to read. It covers everything you need: how the two
buttons work, how to choose a PIN (and why you must not forget it), how to type a password on your
computer, and how to manage everything comfortably from your phone or PC.

---

## 1. The two buttons

You only have two buttons, and the whole app is built around them.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A** (front) | go forward / next / up |
| **B** (side) | go back / previous / down |
| **A** held a moment | validate / open |
| **B** held a moment | go back to the previous screen, or delete |
| **A + B** together | validate (the same as holding A on most screens) |

A "long press" is just a bit under half a second. You will get the feel for it very quickly.

Every screen follows this idea. If you ever feel lost: **hold B** to go back.

### The third button: power

Besides A and B, the stick has a small **power button** on its side. It is not used to navigate: it is
only there to switch the stick on and off.

- **Switch on**: press the power button.
- **Switch off**: **double click** the power button, at any time. The owl falls asleep and the stick
  shuts down.

### Switching off

You can switch the stick off in several ways:

- **Double click the power button**, from any screen.
- Choose **Quit** in the menu, or **hold B** in the menu.
- **Keep holding B** for about three seconds, from anywhere.
- Unplug the USB cable, if **Off on unplug** is enabled in the settings.
- Do nothing on the PIN screen for about ten seconds.

When the stick switches off, the vault is locked. Your PIN is asked again the next time.

## 2. First launch

1. A small owl says hello. Press **A** to continue.
2. A QR code leads you to this page. Press **A** again when you are done.
3. **Choose your PIN**, then type it a second time to confirm.

### Typing a PIN

The PIN screen shows a small dial at the bottom, with the digits around the current one.

- **A**: next digit
- **B**: previous digit
- **Hold A**: validate this digit and start the next one
- **Hold B**: erase the last digit
- **A + B**: validate the whole PIN

Your PIN must have **6 to 12 digits**. The longer, the better: it is the only thing protecting your
passwords if someone gets hold of the stick.

### ⚠️ Please do not forget your PIN

The PIN is never stored anywhere, not even on the stick. That is what keeps your passwords safe, but it
also means **nobody can recover it, not even me**. If you forget it, the only way out is a full reset,
which erases everything (see section 9).

Write it down somewhere safe, or pick something you will remember.

---

## 3. Unlocking

Each time you switch the stick on, type your PIN the same way, then validate with **A + B**.

> Wrong PIN? You get a short message and you can simply try again.

---

## 4. Moving around

After the PIN you land on your **list of passwords**. The most recently used ones are at the top, and
the first line is always **+ Add Password**.

- **A**: go down
- **B**: go up
- **Hold A**: open the selected entry (or create a new one on *Add Password*)
- **Hold B**: open the **menu**

### The menu

A row of squares: **P**assword, **B**ridge, **S**ettings, **Q**uit.

- **A** / **B**: move between them
- **Hold A**: open the one that is selected
- **Hold B**: switch the stick off

---

## 5. Using a password

Open an entry from the list. You will see its fields (username, password, and so on). Secret ones are
hidden behind `*****`.

- **A** / **B**: move between the fields
- **Hold A** on a field: show it in full, to read it
- **A + B** on a field: **type it on the computer** (see below)
- **Hold A** on *Delete*: asks you to confirm, then hold A once more to really delete
- **Hold B**: back to the list

### Typing on your computer

The stick behaves like a USB keyboard.

1. Plug it into the computer.
2. Click in the login or password box on your screen.
3. On the stick, select the field and press **A + B**. It types it for you.

If the characters come out wrong (for example `q` instead of `a`), change **USB keyboard** between
AZERTY and QWERTY in the settings: it has to match the keyboard of the computer you type on.

---

## 6. Adding a password from the stick

Choose **+ Add Password** in the list. It asks, in order, for a **Title**, a **Username/Email**, and
the **Password size**. The password itself is generated for you, random and strong.

For the title and the username, you pick characters one by one with **A** and **B**, **hold A** to add
the character and **hold B** to erase one. **A + B** validates the step.

- If you saved username or email presets on the website (see below), they are offered first when you start the username or email.
- Validate an empty text with **A + B** and the stick gives it a temporary name (ex: `Temp123`). You can
  rename it later from the website.
- For the size, **A** and **B** change the number, then **A + B** creates the password.

Honestly, typing on two buttons is slow. It works, but the website is much more comfortable. And if you do type on the stick, don't forget your presets: they save a lot of time!

## 7. The web portal (Bridge)

This is the easy way to add, edit and organise everything from your phone or computer. Nothing goes
through the internet: the stick creates its own Wi-Fi network, for you only.

1. On the stick, open the menu and choose **Bridge**.
2. The stick shows a **QR code**, the name of its Wi-Fi (like `Bridge-A1F`) and a password.
3. Scan the QR code with your phone, or join the Wi-Fi by hand.
4. The page opens by itself. If it does not, open a browser and go to **http://192.168.4.1**.
5. The stick asks if it can trust your device: **A** accepts, **B** refuses.
6. That is it. You are connected, and only one device can be connected at a time.

On the page you can:

- add, edit and delete passwords, with all the field types (card number, notes, secrets...)
- generate strong passwords
- save **username / email presets**: the ones you use often, offered first when you create an entry on the stick
- change the theme, brightness, keyboard layout, and the other settings
- export or import your passwords from the settings of the page

Your changes are sent to the stick only when you press **Validate**, all at once. Until then nothing
changes, so you can undo or discard freely.

Press **B** on the stick to disconnect, or **hold B** to leave the Bridge.

---

## 8. Settings

From the menu, choose **Settings**. **A** / **B** to move, **hold A** to change a setting.

- **Theme**: the colors of the screen. You see them live while you browse.
- **USB keyboard**: AZERTY or QWERTY.
- **Show PIN digits**: show the digits you have typed, or hide them behind `*`.
- **Brightness**.
- **Auto-lock**: locks the vault after a while without any button press.
- **Shuffle PIN pad**: the dial starts on a random digit each time, so nobody can guess your PIN by
  watching your fingers.
- **Off on unplug**: the stick switches off when you remove the USB cable.
- **Sort**: by most recent use, or alphabetical.

At the bottom, **Change PIN** and **Reset stick**.

---

## 9. When something goes wrong

- **Switching off**: see "Switching off" in section 1.
- **I forgot my PIN**: on the PIN screen, **hold A + B for 10 seconds**. A bar fills the screen. If you
  hold until the end, **everything is erased**: all passwords and the PIN. If you release before, nothing
  happens. You can also do it from *Settings*, with *Reset stick* (hold A for 3 seconds).
- **The computer does not see the keyboard**: use the USB port of the stick directly, and try another
  cable. Some cables only charge.

---

## 10. A word about security

NoxPass aims for *good* security, not magic. Your passwords are encrypted with AES-256, and the key is
protected by your PIN. Without the PIN, a copy of the stick's memory is very hard to open, and a long PIN
makes it much harder. Everything is decrypted in memory only while the stick is unlocked.

It is a personal project and has not been audited. Please keep that in mind, and keep a backup of
the passwords that really matter to you.
